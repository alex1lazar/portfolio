## Designing for calm in a complex industry and what kind of designer are we looking for

### What’s Kota doing?

Benefits are broken on both sides. The people in HR, ops or finance spend their weeks on manual reconciliation, broker emails, ancient provider portals, and answering their employees’ questions in a field they have no expertise in.

Most employees don’t take full advantage of the benefits offered. Oftentimes, they don’t know what they have until something goes wrong: an injury, a sick child, or a checkup they’ve been putting off. Core benefits like health, pension, salary sacrifice or life assurance are extremely complex and employees shouldn’t spend time learning about them.

Kota’s fixing that. We’re giving time back to the people responsible for offering benefits. And their employees get clarity, easy access, and the information to actually use them: for the security and lifestyle they were meant to provide.

## How do we think about design at Kota?

I’m Alex, the founding designer at Kota, and up until recently, I’ve been the only designer working across product, marketing, web, and branding. Over the last 3 years, some things have become clearer and clearer about what works for the team and where we’re trying to go.

### **Design drives alignment**

We’re small, so writing and designing are the ways we think together. Jumping into design early has helped us understand where we need to discover more information, scope the engineering effort, and accept the concessions needed to ship the projects by deadlines. 

### **Design as a system**

Decisions in our products affect our customers’ payroll and finances, and their employees’ savings and insurance. For our platforms, we treat design more like infrastructure than marketing while still finding ways to inject our brand language. Designers here need a 360 view of what’s happening and seek feedback early.

### Uncommon care

As the bar to build our ideas lowers, the products that stand out are those where people truly care about their mission and craft. Demonstrating deep care isn’t about adding shiny or trendy effects. It’s often redoing a flow that you know it could be better, improving a page that you shipped to meet a deadline but it’s below your standard, or sacrificing design time when your teammates need a hand.

### **Push back is expected**

We don’t believe PMs, designers, or engineers own the solutions. Shipping a new project is a collaborative effort in which all people involved are open to and offer feedback. We expect and seek these moments of push back. Everyone here is considered a Product person, all striving toward a common goal: creating the ultimate benefits experience for companies and their employees.

## What do we expect from this designer?

### **A doer and a builder**

Lines between PMs, designers, and engineers are getting blurry lately. This role is for a top IC designer who thinks beyond just design work. You default to thinking about the full product cycle, interface design being only part of the process. You collaborate easily across departments to create strong project specs, explore solutions, and ship the best thing possible.

### **Complexity to simplicity, with care**

You thrive on thinking through complex problem statements and looking for ways to deliver a simple solution. Understanding user feedback, iterating on different design solutions, pushing the boundaries of the team’s designs, staying close to engineers during build, and keeping an eye on metrics post-launch. An all-rounder, obsessed with crafting great software experiences.

### **Beyond good enough**

In a time when AI is putting out decent design work, libraries are plenty, and most products are starting to look the same, innovation truly happens beyond good enough. If you’re fine pushing beyond the brief, showing new ways of doing things, hyping others about a novel solution, all while working at 400% zoom in Figma, then we’re on the same page.

**In a nutshell, this role is for you if you:**

- Feel confident about the fundamentals of design: type, spacing, rhythm, micro-interactions.
- Get excited drawing flows, finding edge cases, and covering error states.
- Are comfortable exploring numerous iterations and throwing away your beloved idea.
- Design with flows, copy, and aesthetics in mind by default.
- Can explain your decisions through design fundamentals and psychology.
- Work well across departments.
- Have a strong portfolio with a high bar for interface design and evidence of the thinking behind the work.
